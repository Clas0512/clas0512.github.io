Raycaster

The algorithm behind Cub3D, a 42 Istanbul pair project, rewritten in JavaScript for this site. For each column of the screen, one ray steps through the map grid cell by cell (DDA) until it hits a wall. The distance to that hit decides how tall the column's wall slice is drawn.

  • WASD move
  • ←→ turn
  • idle autopilot takes over
Engineering notes

Collision Movement is resolved one axis at a time, so pushing into a wall at an angle slides along it, and each step is far smaller than a cell, so the player cannot tunnel. Walls use perpendicular distance, which removes the fisheye effect.

Verify inspect() returns a read-only snapshot: position, angle, autopilot state and ray count.

—

Verlet physics

Verlet integration moves each point from its current and previous position instead of storing a velocity, then constraints pull the points back into shape. It is the same idea games use for ragdolls, rope and cloth.

  • drag throw a body
  • click / tap empty space to add a ball
  • SpaceEnter drop a ball
  • R reset the scene
Engineering notes

Stability Fixed 120 Hz step with 8 constraint passes, at most 8 steps per frame after a stall, and a hard speed cap.

Verify inspect() reports non-finite values, points outside the walls, the deepest circle overlap and the top speed: conditions a test can assert on.

Arcade

Three small games written for this site on one shared kit: a fixed 60 Hz simulation loop, a state machine (ready → playing ⇄ paused → game over / won) that refuses illegal transitions, auto-pause when the tab loses focus, and an in-memory fallback when localStorage is blocked. Each one opens in a viewer.

  • Snake

    The classic: eat, grow longer, and don't run into yourself.

    Controls arrow keys or WASD, or swipe

    Engineering notes

    Edge cases Turns are buffered and checked against the last queued direction, so two quick turns both register and a 180° reversal is refused. Moving into the tail cell is legal because it is vacated that tick. Food only spawns on free cells, and a full board ends as a win.

    Verify The rules live in a DOM-free Board with an injectable random source, exposed on window.__game for automated checks.

  • Breakout

    Bounce the ball off your paddle and clear the wall of bricks.

    Controls ← → or mouse, touch drag, Space to launch

    Engineering notes

    Collision The ball moves in sub-steps of at most half its radius and is tested against the closest point on each brick, so it cannot pass through a brick even at top speed.

    Verify window.__game exposes the ball, bricks, lives, setSpeed, moveBall and loadLevel, so a check can force top speed and step the ball directly.

  • Pong

    Two paddles, one ball. You play against the computer.

    Controls W / S or ↑ / ↓, touch drag; on a tall screen the court turns vertical: ← → or A / D

    Engineering notes

    Edge cases The simulation always runs on a horizontal court; on a tall screen only rendering and input are rotated, and turning the device mid-game pauses it. The computer is imperfect by design: it rechecks the ball a few times a second, aims with random error and has a capped speed.

    Collision Ball sub-steps are capped at half its radius, so it cannot skip over a paddle.
